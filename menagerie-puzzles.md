# The Menagerie — Two Puzzles

GM prep. Party: **Anzu** (Tengu Psychic, familiar Othello), **Argyle** (Elf Cleric), **Benny** (Android Fighter), **Johnny** (Human Bard). All level 8.

**Context:** the tire yard and the Menagerie sit on top of a rift into the Black and White, a world of cognition where ideas create reality. The party has been to Noir already. Puzzle 1 gets them out of the yard (solved from the car). Puzzle 2 gets them past the city limits (solved on foot).

**Design rule inherited from Noir:** the solution is physically present before the players know it matters. Show the props early and unremarked; the satisfaction is retroactive.

---

# Puzzle 1 — What the Car Believes

## The core rule

The yard is not a place, it's a **null result** — where the Black and White files vehicles whose destination is incoherent. The car carries five separate claims about where it's going, and they contradict each other, so it goes nowhere forever.

The rule the players need to discover:

> **Blank is permitted. Contradiction is not.**

They do **not** need all five artifacts to say "the Menagerie." They need to eliminate every contradiction. Neutralizing four and setting one correctly works exactly as well as solving all five. Finding that shortcut should feel like winning, not cheating — reward it.

## The five claims

| # | Artifact | Where | Currently says | To satisfy it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The manifest** — a delivery clipboard | Clipped to the dash | A destination scratched out and overwritten until illegible | Write a destination. But it's a *delivery* manifest — it implies cargo. Claim a destination with an empty trunk and the sheet goes blank on its own |
| 2 | **The atlas** — a road map | Glovebox | Roads that all curve back. The Menagerie is on no page | Find a page, postcard, or hand-drawn map containing the city, and add it |
| 3 | **The radio** | Dash | Static, or a station that only ever says *"continue straight"* | Tune to a frequency that gives real directions |
| 4 | **The ornament** — a figure on the hood | Hood | Points dead ahead — i.e. wherever the car is *already* going. The loop, made into an object | Turn it, unscrew it, or break it off |
| 5 | **The plate / decal** | Bumper | A yard-issued salvage tag. **This is why the yard claims them** — by its own paperwork this car is already scrap | Replace it, or pry it off |

**Blanking each one** is trivial: tear off the manifest sheet, throw the atlas out the window, smash the radio, snap off the ornament, pry off the plate. The insight isn't *how* — it's *that you may*.

## Costs of solving properly (rather than blanking)

- **Manifest** — needs cargo. Whatever goes in the trunk becomes what they're delivering, and they arrive at the Menagerie *as a delivery*. That's a real claim on entry (see `FREIGHT`, Puzzle 2). But the city expects to receive it.
- **Atlas** — holds a fixed number of real places. Adding the Menagerie **erases the page they came in on.** No going back the way they came.
- **Radio** — the station broadcasting Menagerie directions is *of* the Menagerie. Tuning in means something on the other end hears the car. They arrive expected.
- **Ornament** — turning it means the car's own "forward" is now behind it. Some stretch of the drive happens in reverse, or the yard runs backward around them. Strand them somewhere strange once.
- **Plate** — whatever the new plate says, the world treats them as. A hearse plate makes them a hearse, and things start flagging them down with bodies.

## The wrecks are the tutorial

Failed attempts, frozen mid-solution. Reading them teaches the rule without anyone explaining it.

| Wreck | What's in it | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Station wagon, doors open | Manifest reads a clear destination in firm handwriting. Atlas missing entirely. Radio smashed. **Four neutralized, one set** — but the ornament still points forward | The near-miss. One contradiction was enough |
| Hearse, upright, undamaged | Everything blank. Nothing contradicts. Nothing states anything | Blank alone isn't enough — you need *one* positive claim. Also: the hearse plate is here for the taking |
| Delivery van | Manifest names the Menagerie in full. Trunk empty. The sheet is visibly going blank as they watch | The cargo rule, demonstrated live |
| Sedan, skeleton at the wheel | Hand gripping a torn atlas page showing a city | The map page they need, and a warning |

The skeleton's page is the single most important prop. Let them find it early and not understand what it's for.

## Clue ladder

Roughly one per lap, until someone bites:

1. **The car resists.** Hard turns off-road curve back to the roadway — not violently, politely, the way a shopping cart pulls.
2. **The radio speaks and the car obeys.** The station says *"continue straight,"* and the wheel eases straight under the driver's hands. First time it's ambiguous. Second time it isn't.
3. **A wreck, examined.** Any of the above.
4. **The plate.** Someone reads their own bumper and finds a yard-issued salvage tag that was not there when they got in. *The yard filed them.*

## Party hooks

- **Anzu (Psychic)** — listens to the car, gets the impression of an animal given conflicting commands that has given up. The whole solution delivered as a feeling.
- **Johnny (Bard, occult)** — can interrogate the radio. The station has a DJ. The DJ can be talked to, and lies.
- **Benny (Android)** — is himself a machine with a manifest. Let him spot the salvage tag first; he knows what being classified as equipment feels like.
- **Argyle (Cleric, divine)** — reads the yard as unconsecrated in the *bureaucratic* sense. Nothing is wrong here. Nothing is anything here.

## Tying it to the fight

The scrap-things escalate per lap — not more of them, but **the same ones reassembling from the wrecks the party keeps searching.** Looting becomes a cost, the combat feels like the yard defending itself, and you get a pacing dial with no visible clock.

# Puzzle 2 — The Gate

Two layers, discovered in this order:

1. **The lock:** the city admits one of each kind. The party collides. They must deduce *what scheme* the gate sorts by.
2. **The key:** the only way to change your kind is a placard. Placards are finite, all currently in use, and taking one breaks something.

## Layer 1 — The taxonomy

The Menagerie sorts by **what you are made of** and **what you are like** — material and temperament. It's a specimen collection; it thinks like a taxidermist. The collection wants one of each on **both** axes.

Run the turnstile as an announcement:

| | Material | Temperament |
|---|---|---|
| Anzu | FEATHER | WARY |
| Benny | ALLOY | PREDATORY |
| Argyle | MEAT | DOCILE |
| Johnny | MEAT | WARY |
| Othello | FEATHER | DOCILE |

Four collisions across five bodies: MEAT ×2, FEATHER ×2, WARY ×2, DOCILE ×2. **Benny is the only clean one** — a nice beat for the android, the one made of the rarest stuff walking straight in.

Othello is the wrinkle with teeth: does a familiar count as its own specimen, or as part of its master? The gate says its own thing. Anzu has to reclassify him, leave him, or argue the metaphysics.

Deduction should be *fast*. The scheme isn't the puzzle; the fix is. If they're floundering after two guesses, have the Docent give it away.

## The Docent — the oracle

A figure in a worn uniform who addresses everything, including people, in exhibit-label prose. *"Note the specimen's characteristic posture of alarm."*

**Critical function:** the Docent honestly and precisely answers any question of the form *"what is this?"* — but never *"what should we do?"* It classifies. It does not advise.

This is the anti-stall valve. The party can walk the plaza asking the Docent to classify anything — themselves, their gear, a placard, a pigeon, the gate — and test every hypothesis without burning attempts at the turnstile. Both attributes are cast on each brass plate, but they have to go read them.

Its one blind spot: it cannot classify itself. Asked, it changes the subject.

## Layer 2 — The placards

Every object in the Menagerie wears a small brass placard. In the Black and White the placard doesn't describe the thing, it **causes** it. Placards unscrew. There is no spare stock, no blank brass, no foundry inside the walls — every placard in reach is currently doing a job.

| Placard | Mounted on | Cost of taking it |
|---|---|---|
| `STONE · DORMANT` | The lion statue at the gate | The lion stops being stone. It has held that pose a long time and is furious |
| `SMOKE · FUGITIVE` | The perpetually burning barrel | The smoke disperses, revealing what it was hiding — a body, a door, or a person standing very still |
| `GLASS · FRAGILE` | A display case, contents indistinct | The contents become loose and real |
| `PAPER · DOCILE` | The plaza notice board | Every posted notice in the district goes blank at once — including anything the party wrote down |
| `WAX · DOCILE` | A seated figure in a booth, lifelike | It melts. Whoever it was modeled on feels it happen, wherever they are, and starts moving toward the gate |
| `WATER · DORMANT` | The fountain | The fountain stops. Something was being kept submerged, for a reason |
| *(blank)* | An empty pedestal | **Nothing.** You are nothing. You pass unremarked — and the city does not hold you, because it isn't holding anything. Drift. Fall out. Be somewhere else |

**House rules, if asked:**

- Taking a placard from an *object* is bad. Taking one from a *creature* is much worse — the creature becomes undefined, which here means it becomes whatever is nearby and hungry.
- You cannot write your own. The city honors cast brass only.
- A placard worn is a placard *worn*. It can be taken off you as easily as you took it.

## The solution space

Minimum is **two placards** — one MEAT and one FEATHER must break, and both new plates must also avoid WARY, PREDATORY, and DOCILE, which are already spoken for.

**Working pairs** (fixing Johnny and Othello, leaving the others alone):

- `STONE · DORMANT` + `SMOKE · FUGITIVE` ✓
- `STONE · DORMANT` + `GLASS · FRAGILE` ✓
- `SMOKE · FUGITIVE` + `GLASS · FRAGILE` ✓

**Dead ends** that look fine until you check the second column:

- `PAPER · DOCILE` — collides with Argyle
- `WAX · DOCILE` — same
- `STONE · DORMANT` + `WATER · DORMANT` — both DORMANT, collide with each other

`PAPER` is a genuine trap rather than a flavorful freebie. If they still want it for Johnny — and they might, it suits a bard — they must *also* move Argyle off `DOCILE`, meaning a third placard and a third broken thing. Their call.

**The blank placard** is the trap and the best option simultaneously. It solves the gate perfectly and has the worst consequence. Let a clever player find it and take it. Don't warn them beyond the Docent's flat, accurate classification: *"Unoccupied. Pending acquisition."*

## Other ways through

1. **Reclassify the collection instead of yourselves.** The gate says "we have one." Go find the one they have — somewhere in the Menagerie is the specimen currently occupying `MEAT`. Change *its* placard and the slot opens. Softer puzzle, darker and more on-point: the party edits the collection to make room for themselves, and whatever they overwrite doesn't get a vote. Good escape hatch if the grid gets fiddly.
2. **Argue Othello isn't FEATHER.** Familiars are made of their master's magic. A well-made case might get the Docent to concede the familiar is `IDEA`, which the collection notably lacks. Reward the metaphysical argument — it's the most Black-and-White solution available.
3. **The `FREIGHT` route.** If they did the manifest work in the yard and arrived as a delivery, one of them rides in the trunk and enters as **cargo, not visitor.** Cargo isn't a specimen and isn't sorted by material at all. This is the reward for solving Puzzle 1 thoroughly instead of cheaply — a door that only opened because of a choice made an hour ago.
4. **Somebody stays out.** Always available. The gate isn't the only way in, and a PC left outside the walls generates its own session.

## The link back

Make the connection explicit **only after** they solve it. The moment someone says *"wait, is that why the manifest needed cargo?"* is the payoff for the whole two-puzzle structure. If they blanked their way through the yard they never see path 3 and never know it was there — that's what makes the thorough path worth taking next time.
